1  What happens — and the two ways it shows itself

System 2 has been seen behaving in two distinct ways. Both have been present since the record begins, but which one dominates has changed twice — on 3 July and again on 12 August. The two look completely different in the measurements, and only one of them leaves a record in the fault log. Everything else in this summary is labelled by mode so it is always clear which is meant.

Mode A — it trips, and it tells you

The inverter reports a "Grid power outage" (code 10), followed 7–9 seconds later by a "System fault" (code 329), and clears itself in under a minute. The building keeps its solar — the inverter goes on serving the load throughout. Every occurrence is timestamped to the second in the portal fault log.

Dominant: 1 June – 2 July — 96 code-10 records on 21 days  ·  returned on 12 August (5 records)
Absent: 3 July – 11 August — zero records in 40 days

Mode B — it drops out quietly

AC output falls to 0.00 kW while the array is still generating and the grid meter reads normal. The building's solar supply stops until it recovers, typically within a few minutes. Nothing is written to the fault log at all — it is only visible in the measurement channels.

Dominant: 3 July – 10 August — 81 events, 21.4 per 1,000 chances
Present but rare before that: 9 events in the 48 days from 14 May, 2.6 per 1,000 — an eightfold difference

Neither mode is "islanding" Islanding requires the mains supply to have been lost. The meter reads normal at every event in both modes, so no island can have formed. The accurate description of Mode B is that System 2 ceases exporting while the grid is present; Mode A is an inverter fault raised and cleared while the grid is present.

Stated precisely, because the distinction matters. Mode B was present but rare through June — 4 events, 2.0 per 1,000 chances — and across the whole pre-July record (14 May – 30 June) 9 events at 2.6 per 1,000. It then rose to 21.4, an eightfold increase. Mode A is the cleaner signal: 96 records over 21 days to 2 July, then exactly zero for 40 days, then a return. The chart starts at 1 June because no fault list exists before then, so May cannot be classified by mode at all.

The decline levelled off — and has not resumed Over the last fourteen days (30 July – 12 August): System 2 had 11 events, System 1 had 1, from almost identical opportunity (1,363 vs 1,347 exposed intervals). One-sided test on that split: p = 0.003. The most recent six days run above the preceding five (8.59 vs 4.13 per 1,000), so the July decline has stopped rather than continued — and 12 August's zero reflects a change of mode, not an improvement.

Two independent records — and they barely overlap (July)

Estimated true count: ~145 events in July Two incomplete records of the same events, overlapping on only 8, allow the total to be estimated (capture–recapture): ≈145, 95% CI 85–205. The five-minute data catches about half; the notification channel about one in nine. The method's known biases here point downward, so ~145 is likely conservative — and it agrees with the directly timed event in panel 8.

Counting note: the two records are matched on 15-minute windows. One notification window can contain two counted events, and two notified events fall below the 2 kW counting gate, which is why the circle totals do not simply add to 72 and 17.

Notification timestamps are batched — they are not event times All 20 notifications to date — 17 in July and 3 on 10 August — land on an exact quarter hour (:00, :15, :30, :45) with a seconds value of 00 or 01. The chance of that happening naturally is 9 × 10−54. They are a scheduled send; the event lies somewhere in the preceding 15 minutes. Any record that quotes these times — ours included — describes a 15-minute window, not a moment.

6  The two inverters are in different operating states — unrelated to Mode A / Mode B

Both device pages carry a field labelled Operating status. They do not match. Verified directly on iSolarCloud, 7 August 2026.

SystemOperating statusCurrent status
System 1 Dispatched runningNormal
System 2 Grid-connected operationNormal

Grid-connected operation is ordinary grid-tied running under the inverter's own settings. Dispatched running is defined in Sungrow's own communication protocol for this inverter family — register 13000, value 0x8200:

Sungrow's definition, verbatim "Dispatch Running: the inverter runs according to the scheduling instructions received from the monitoring background. The active power is set to less than 100%."

By that definition it is a curtailment state. System 1 appears to be following scheduling instructions with its active power capped, while System 2 runs unconstrained. The portal label is presumed to map to this register state — the local register read confirms it either way — and what issues the instructions (a portal or installer setting, or an external party) is not yet known.
Two identical inverters, two different modes Same site, same grid connection, same model, same firmware — and one is being externally scheduled and capped while the other is not. This is the only configuration difference so far identified between the two systems, and it is not yet known what is issuing the instructions, or why to only one inverter.
Stated plainly: this is not offered as the cause It is System 1 — the system that is not disconnecting — that is being curtailed, and no evidence links this to the System 2 events. It is raised because it is an unexplained asymmetry between two systems the whole comparison assumes are identical, and because a power-capped inverter makes an imperfect baseline for any comparison of output or curtailment behaviour.

Ask on the day: what is issuing scheduling instructions to System 1, and to what active-power limit? Is it deliberate, and who configured it? Is it persistent or only while an instruction is active? Should both inverters be in the same mode — and if so, which one is wrong? Read registers 13000 and 13050 on both units locally — the app is documented to misreport EMS mode, so the portal display cannot settle this.
