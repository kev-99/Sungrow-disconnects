Evidence summary · prepared 6 August 2026

System 2 disconnection events

12-14 Richmond Ave, Dee Why NSW · two identical 26 kW Sungrow SH20T systems on one grid connection · System 1 is the comparison throughout · period analysed 1 July – 5 August 2026

System 2 repeatedly stops exporting while its array is generating and the grid is present and normal. It has done so 75 times in 36 days. System 1, on the same supply, has done so 8 times — and not once since 24 July.

The fault log has recorded nothing since 2 July. Notifications stopped on 25 July. The events did not stop — the reporting did.

This is not "islanding" Islanding requires the mains supply to have been lost. The meter reads normal at every single event, so no island can have formed. The accurate description is that System 2 ceases exporting while the grid is present.

The decline stopped — it did not reach zero Over 29 July – 5 August: System 2 had 6 events, System 1 had 0, from almost identical opportunity (780 vs 761 exposed intervals). One-sided test on that split: p = 0.017. The difference in the most recent period is statistically significant, not chance.

Silence is not health No fault has been logged since 2 July and no notification issued since 25 July — across a period containing 75 measured events, seven of them after the last notification.

Two independent records — and they barely overlap

Estimated true count: ~145 events in July Two incomplete records of the same events, overlapping on only 8, allow the total to be estimated (capture–recapture): ≈145, 95% CI 85–205. The five-minute data catches about half; the notification channel about one in nine. The method under-counts here, so 145 is a floor.
Notification timestamps are batched — they are not event times All 17 July notifications land on an exact quarter hour (:00, :15, :30, :45) with a seconds value of 00 or 01. The chance of that happening naturally is 8 × 10−46. They are a scheduled send; the event lies somewhere in the preceding 15 minutes. Any record quoting these times — including the installer's — describes a window, not a moment.

The two remaining causes look identical in all monitoring data An inverter-side internal trip and an uncommanded contactor dropout produce exactly the same signature: meter normal, terminals depressed, backup port collapsed, no fault logged. No amount of further portal data can separate them.
One physical test would separate them Measure the System 2 contactor coil voltage at the coil, under load, and the auxiliary contact resistance. This has never been done. Because the inverter's own command outputs are proven never to operate, this is the only remaining discriminator.

7  For the site attendance — Friday 14 August 2026

  1. Test the System 2 contactor coil circuit — coil voltage at the coil under load, and aux contact resistance. Never performed. The only remaining way to identify the fault. Make this the priority of the visit.
  2. Do not plan around observing an event on the day. At ~0.7 events per generating day, a full day on site 09:00–16:00 has about a 51% chance of catching one; a 10:00–14:00 attendance about 34%. Observing nothing proves nothing. A recording power-quality logger left for 2–3 weeks would be far more informative.
  3. Obtain the inverter-side event log from Sungrow. Both customer-visible channels are silent while events continue. Protection operations that raise no customer fault should appear in Sungrow's own record.
  4. Obtain, in writing, the protection threshold values before and after 1 July. Events began 3 July, two days after those settings were changed. Without the values the relationship cannot be assessed.
  5. Ask what stops the inverter exporting without raising a logged fault. The log has been silent through an estimated ~145 events. Also: why did notifications stop on 25 July while events continued?
